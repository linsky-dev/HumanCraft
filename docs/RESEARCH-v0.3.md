# Research-to-engineering delta

The four-volume Norman collection informs the existing modules. Re-examination of volume 1 ch. 5 separates action slips, memory lapses and mistaken plans; volume 2 ch. 8 motivates past/current/future context, cross-system consistency and learning; volume 4 ch. 6 motivates truthful shared state and real control. Volume 3's three experience levels remain a constraint: aesthetics cannot compensate for unsafe or inaccessible behavior. The new playbook maps these principles to conditional remedies and falsifiable tests. It is not a reproduction of the books.

Modern sources (see skill references/sources.md) support task-local, version-aware instructions, independent acceptance criteria and activation positives/negatives. SkillsBench and SWE-Skills-Bench motivate matched trials rather than assuming all skills improve outcomes. The latter also motivates checking stack/version conflicts. GOV.UK and Playwright motivate realistic tasks, user-visible assertions and representative accessibility testing. Earlier hackathon-derived lessons retained: independent activation, useful demos, evidence-first diagnosis, low setup burden and reproducibility.

New engineering work: risk routing, decision playbook, frontend delivery, single-effect trace checker, positive/negative unit tests, 24 activation cases and scoring utility. The checker cannot establish completeness or real-world reliability. The release gate still requires actual matched model experiments and human testing. Do not claim market ratings or broad compatibility before testing.
